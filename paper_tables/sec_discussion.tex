\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\noindent\textbf{Reproduction.} Baselines were rerun with independent PSNR/SSIM implementations agreeing with the reference ones to machine precision. CIDNet and Retinexformer match published scores within \(0.021\)\,dB except two cells, \(\nUnresHi{}\) and \(\nUnresLo{}\)\,dB, both the rectified cell of one CIDNet variant; SNR-Net, LLFormer and URetinex-Net at its reference-derived ratio match LOL within \(0.003\)\,dB; the diffusion gaps are \(0.13\) and \(0.34\)\,dB, the latter inside a \nLDspread{}\,dB seed spread that exceeds its anchored gain; Zero-DCE++ has no published LOL score.

\noindent\textbf{What the decomposition implies for method design.} The headroom on Sony is \nHRglob{}\,dB for a global gain, \nHRchan{} more per channel, the block and affine values of Fig.~\ref{fig:workflow}, \nHRbandls{} for per-band amplitude and about \nHRnoise{} for the noise share. Spend effort on the largest headroom after testing its predictors: the anchored correction does this for the global term, while the amplitude boost lowers PSNR and our low-frequency predictors leave most of the block oracle unused. Training on $k$-frame means is the next candidate.

\noindent\textbf{Limitations.} All quantities describe the benchmarks' 8-bit sRGB renditions. Quantisation and non-additive sensor noise~\cite{eld2020} depart from the model behind the correction: its check fails in the lowest band at $0.04$ and $0.1$\,s, and $N_j/P_j>\nNPthresh{}$ cells are reported as unstable. Correlation is distortion-oriented~\cite{blau2018}: neither low correlation nor a failed predictor establishes information absence. The reference's noise floor is treated as zero. Noise correction needs repeated captures, so it exists only on Sony. The band-wise test covers Retinexformer on Sony and two models on LOL; CIDNet's Sony residual was not decomposed because its training variant uses another pixel convention. The correction is one division, neither uniform nor portable: it acts only on unsaturated outputs, harms most models where the precondition fails, and cannot be validated on training scenes, where the oracle gain is near one. Pooled band correlations can hide local disagreement, and the multi-frame result uses static scenes only.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Extreme-dark scores are protocol-bound: four choices move them, the largest by nearly ten decibels,
so we reran eighteen pairs under one protocol and priced three of them as differences. Component-wise
oracles, equal-error controls and band-wise diagnostics then say which term to attack: the
low-frequency term is common to all and resists our predictors, the global term is a generalisation
gap. From that follows one correction with a checkable precondition, worth \nFRetgain{}\,dB on Sony
and costing \nLOLRFloss{}\,dB where it fails; both are reported.
