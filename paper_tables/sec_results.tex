\section{Experimental results}
\label{sec:results}

\noindent\textbf{Setup.} Every table and figure applies Sec.~\ref{sec:method} to released checkpoints, training-free baselines or one in-house model. The Sony sRGB extreme-dark benchmark derives from SID~\cite{sid2018} in the processed form prepared for SNR-Net~\cite{snrnet2022} and released with Retinexformer~\cite{retinexformer}: \nNSCENE{} test scenes, \nNIMG{} frames at $512\times960$, each scored against its scene's single long-exposure frame. LOL~\cite{lol2018} ($15$ frames) with its v2 sets~\cite{lolv2_2021} is the standard low-light benchmark. The model analysed is the released Retinexformer checkpoint; inference is deterministic, so spreads are over frames or scenes. We reproduce its reported score
to within \(0.002\) dB with our own metric implementation (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\noindent\textbf{Controlled diagnostic validation.} On \nCTRLn{} Sony references, the procedure separates a global gain
from a smooth spatial gain of identical per-image MSE (global least squares removes \nCTRLglob{} versus \nCTRLspglob{}
percent of the error), and a reversible circular shift preserves power yet gives high-band correlation \nCTRLshift{}: low
correlation is not information loss.

\noindent\textbf{Four protocol choices move the score by decibels.} First,
reference-mean rectification sets the output's brightness from the reference~\cite{evlightpp2024}: over the \nSWn{} method--benchmark pairs, it is worth \nSWlo{} to \nSWhi{}\,dB, the top end on a training-free baseline and \nSWtrLo{} to \nSWtrHi{} across the \nSWtrN{} pairs whose model was trained on paired references.
One method recommends against the setting while another reports its headline with it, so the two are incomparable. Our anchor is the 8-bit ceiling, not a statistic of the evaluated scene. Second, the 8-bit
rule: one pipeline truncates and another rounds; switching it alone is worth \nBITlo{} to \nBIThi{}\,dB on
the three LOL checkpoints. Third, how PSNR is aggregated: the frame mean exceeds the PSNR of the pooled MSE, necessarily by AM--GM~\cite{psnragg2021}, and here by \nAGlo{} to \nAGhi{}\,dB. Fourth, the dataset variant: the Sony set is distributed in at least two renditions whose transfer functions differ~\cite{retinexformer,cidnet,cidnetplus}, one built with no gamma curve, and the checkpoint
of~\cite{cidnet} scores \nCROSSpsnr{}\,dB on the other, an absolute score rather than a difference, against the \(\nCIDreported{}\) reported on its own.

\noindent\textbf{Existing methods under one protocol, and the proposed decomposition
applied to each.} Table~\ref{tab:decomp} scores, with rounding and no rectification, \nCMPnLOL{} methods on LOL and \nCMPnSony{} on Sony: CLAHE~\cite{clahe1994}, Zero-DCE++~\cite{zerodcepp2022}, SCI~\cite{sci2022}, an in-house self-supervised Retinex model (AR), LightenDiffusion~\cite{lightendiff2024} (unpaired; no Sony results reported), and reference-trained networks (URetinex-Net~\cite{uretinex2022}, SNR-Net~\cite{snrnet2022}, LLFormer~\cite{llformer2023},
the diffusion model GSAD~\cite{gsad2023}, Retinexformer, CIDNet~\cite{cidnet}); the rightmost four columns are Sec.~\ref{sec:method} applied to each method's own residual; Fig.~\ref{fig:cmp} shows
one frame. On LOL, \nCMPclearLOL{} methods clear $20$\,dB unrectified, the rest staying below \nCMPrestLOL{}\,dB; on Sony only the two trained on it do, and LightenDiffusion leaves \nLDsonyChan{} percent of its error in the per-channel term, a colour cast. Across those methods (bounds rounded outward from the unrounded shares) the global term takes \nCMPglobLOLlo{}--\nCMPglobLOLhi{} percent of the error on LOL and \nCMPglobSonylo{}--\nCMPglobSonyhi{} on Sony, so which term dominates varies with method and benchmark, while the block oracle stays at \nCMPdLOLlo{}--\nCMPdLOLhi{}\,dB on LOL and \nCMPdSonylo{}--\nCMPdSonyhi{}\,dB on Sony: the spatially varying low-frequency term is common to all.

\noindent\textbf{Global tone and colour: bounded LS corrections.} On Sony, an oracle global gain and per-channel gain account for \nSHAREglob{} and
\nSHAREchan{} percent of Retinexformer's squared error, taking the score from \nPSNRmodel{} to \nAFchan{} dB;
the affine map of~\cite{pal2026} takes \nAFshare{} percent, \nAFover{}\,dB more; the remaining \nSHAREresid{} percent is the unclipped residual after the per-channel LS optimum, growing from \nSHAREresidLong{} percent at $0.1$\,s to \nSHAREresidShort{} at $0.033$\,s.

\noindent\textbf{Raw and noise-adjusted diagnostics.} Raw input correlation is below the model's in every band and exposure (Table~\ref{tab:rho}); below-floor bands hold \nRAWunshort{} percent of residual energy at $0.033$\,s, and noise adjustment labels \nSCALrecshort{}, \nSCALrecmid{} and \nSCALreclong{} percent as input advantage. Both are corner bands, gaps \nGapmid{} and \nGaplong{}, and the $0.04$\,s cell is unstable at $N_j/P_j=\nNPmidCorner{}$. Scene resampling (\nBootSpecB{} draws, seed \nBootSpecSeed{}) retains them in only \nLabelmidPct{}\% and \nLabellongPct{}\% of resamples, so these labels are uncertain.
A repeated-capture check fails at the lowest band at $0.04$ and $0.1$\,s and was not run at $0.033$\,s, so noise adjustment remains a model-dependent sensitivity.

\noindent\textbf{Input and output averaging change different components.} We test
averaging $k$ input frames. On
the \nMFn{} scene--exposure groups with at least eight frames ($\nMFlongN{}$ at $0.1$\,s, $\nMFmidN{}$ at $0.04$\,s), so that every $k$ is scored on identical
groups, this raises the low-frequency error energy after the oracle global gain
monotonically, to \nMFlfb{}, \nMFlfd{} and \nMFlfh{} times the single-frame value at
$k=2$, $4$ and $8$, where pure variance would have given $1/k$, while the high-frequency
energy falls only to \nMFhfh{}. On the \nMFlongN{} scenes at $0.1$\,s, the eight-frame mean's raw score falls \nMFdropRe{}\,dB while the input's band correlation rises in every band, at the corner \nMFinFixA{} to \nMFinFixB{}: high-frequency agreement improves as low-frequency error grows. Averaging outputs instead of inputs gains \nMFoutGain{}\,dB at $k=8$; the noise-realisation share is \nNOISEshare{} percent, \nNOISEdb{}\,dB, under independence. A noise-variance frequency-domain merge~\cite{hdrplus2016} at $k=8$ recovers \nHPdFB{}\,dB of what the mean loses, and re-injecting Gaussian noise does not reverse the growth.


\noindent\textbf{What carries over to the second benchmark.} Three findings carry over to LOL: the output again closer to a blurred reference; the low-frequency part again dominant, \nLOLlfLo{} to \nLOLlfHi{} percent of
the error energy; and the block oracle again above a decibel ($\Delta_{16}$ in Table~\ref{tab:decomp}). On LOL, a control fitted to another image's residual gives \nLPcidPerm{}
and \nLPretPerm{}\,dB, \nLPcidPct{} and \nLPretPct{} percent of the oracle gains.
Hygiene check: no LOL band shows an input advantage against the raw input for either model.

\noindent\textbf{The dominant term is a spatially varying low-frequency field.} The residual after the
global gain is concentrated at low frequency: the low-pass part carries \nLFshare{}
percent of the error energy, of which a single per-frame constant explains only
\nLFconst{} percent. Fig.~\ref{fig:workflow} prices it: one scalar per $16$-pixel block gains \nLADgain{}\,dB over the whole-frame fit, an offset per block \nLADoff{}\,dB and an affine map \nLADaff{}\,dB, after subtracting the negligible structureless control. Residual-donor controls separate same-scene frames from other scenes at matched exposure and residual variance: same-scene donors give \nDonorSame{}\,dB and scene-excluded ones \nDonorCross{}\,dB ($[\nDonorCrossLo{},\nDonorCrossHi{}]$), so, much spatial fit remains for scene-excluded residuals, though the oracle measures a correction family's fit, not an imaging cause. The reference-assisted three-statistic regression of
Sec.~\ref{sec:method}, cross-fitted by scene, reaches $R^{2}=\nRTWOin{}$;
adding the reference block mean, unavailable at inference, raises it to
\nRTWOall{}.
A convolutional predictor on the input frame, split by scene, with failure fixed in advance at $R^{2}<\nTHRrtwo{}$ or a gain below \nTHRdb{}\,dB, reaches $R^{2}=\nCNNrtwo{}$ and \nCNNdb{}\,dB on the output, \nCNNfrac{} percent of the oracle, with a label-shuffled control at or below zero and a single-batch overfit to $R^{2}=0.87$. 

\noindent\textbf{The global term is a generalisation gap, and what the prescribed correction recovers.} The oracle global gain is worth \nHRglob{}\,dB but averages to one per frame, so unconditionally a constant buys \nCconstPool{}\,dB. Its dispersion is a generalisation gap. Brightness lock-in on the training set and the rectification it forces are reported~\cite{clerwkv2026,mill2025}; the standard deviations here are \nAGENtrain{} over training scenes, \nAGENtest{} over test and \nAGENwithin{} within one scene and exposure. Pooled, Retinexformer gains \nFRetgain{}\,dB ([\nFRetlo{}, \nFRethi{}], bootstrap $p=\nFRetp{}$, scene $t$ test \nFptt{}; the signed rank \nFpwil{} drops the \nFnoopSc{} unchanged scenes, so it tests the \nFactSc{} it acts on), SNR-Net \nFSnrgain{} and LightenDiffusion \nFLdgain{}\,dB, Zero-DCE++ moves \nFZdcegain{} at $p=\nFZdcep{}$, and SSIM moves from \nFRetssa{} to \nFRetssb{}. Over the \nFholmM{} pairs, Holm adjustment leaves \nFholmPos{} gains and \nFholmNeg{} harm below $0.05$ (Retinexformer \nFholmRet{}). The precondition predicts the sign for seven of the ten pairs: \nFsatS{} percent of the \nQSATn{} Sony training references saturate at $q=\nFQ{}$ against \nFsatLall{} percent of LOL's \nFlolNimg{}, and there the rule harms \nFlolBad{} of \nFlolN{} models, one by \nFlolWorst{}\,dB, while a badly scaled one gains \nFlolBest{}\,dB. The rule is the identity wherever the output already reaches the ceiling: it multiplies \nFRetact{} of \nNIMG{} frames in \nFactSc{} scenes, so the pooled figure averages over scenes it never changes. On the scenes it changes, the gain is \nFactGain{}\,dB ([\nFactLo{}, \nFactHi{}], two-sided $t$ \nFactT{} and sign test \nFactSign{}, unadjusted and post hoc, \nFactPos{} of \nFactSc{} positive; the best scene holds \nFbestAct{} percent of that mean, the top three \nFtopthreeAct{} percent, and the best scene \nFbestPool{} percent of the scene mean), and the acting mean survives dropping its three best (\nFdropTop{}\,dB, $t$ \nFdropT{}, leave-one-out in [\nFjkLo{}, \nFjkHi{}]). The scene mean does not: its top five scenes hold \nCONtopfive{} percent of it, and dropping the best one and best five leaves \nCONone{} and \nCONfive{}\,dB; nor does the pooled figure (\nCONtopfiveFr{} percent, \nCONoneFr{}, \nCONfiveFr{}). Over all frames of those scenes it is \nFactAllFr{}\,dB per frame, \nFactAll{} by scene. An oracle constant fitted to those frames buys \nCconstAct{} against \nCanchActFr{}\,dB per frame; paired by scene, the difference is \nCpair{} [\nCpairLo{}, \nCpairHi{}], \nCpairPos{} of \nFactSc{} favouring the rule, which is therefore indistinguishable from a reference-fitted constant, to a resolution of \nCTOST{}\,dB (\nCTOSTpct{} percent of the effect), rather than better. Shuffling the multipliers among those frames leaves \nCperm{}\,dB (\nCpermN{} draws). Of the per-frame oracle gain (\nCoraPool{}, \nCoraAct{}\,dB) it recovers \nCrecPool{} and \nCrecAct{} percent. The worst case is \nFwScene{}\,dB over the \nNSCENE{} scenes, \nFwAct{} over the acting ones, \nFwFrame{} for one frame. Splitting all \nNIMG{} frames by exposure---\nFexns{}, \nFexnm{} and \nFexnl{} from \nFexscs{} nested in \nFexscm{} nested in \nFexscl{} scenes---gives \nFexfs{}, \nFexfm{} and \nFexfl{}\,dB, none negative, recombining to the pooled figure; scene $t$ \nFexts{}, \nFextm{} and \nFextl{} on the scene means of the same splits.
