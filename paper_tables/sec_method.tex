\section{Proposed method}
\label{sec:method}

The procedure combines component-specific least-squares (LS) oracles with
input--output reference-correlation diagnostics. Let $y$ be the model output and $g$
the reference, in $[0,255]$. PSNR is computed per frame and averaged; squared-error
shares are pooled over frames. Shares use \emph{unclipped} LS residuals. Reported
global and channel-gain PSNR applies clipping and rounding \emph{after} the LS fit;
it is not an optimum over clipped estimators. Spatial oracle gains use unclipped
PSNR so their matched controls can be subtracted.

\noindent\textbf{Gain terms.} The global fit is
$a^{\star}=\langle y,g\rangle/\langle y,y\rangle$; fitting it separately per channel
adds the colour term. The respective reductions in unclipped squared error define
the global and additional channel shares. We fit a scalar per square block of side
$b$, and separately an offset or affine map. Extra parameters can improve even a
noise fit, so each spatial gain over its whole-frame anchor is corrected by the
gain of the same family against a structureless target of matched variance.

\noindent\textbf{Band terms and aggregation.} We Fourier-transform luminance and
group frequencies into radial bands. After separately fitting each input or output
frame's global brightness gain, let $P_{ij}$ and $G_{ij}$ be its and the reference's
band powers, and $C_{ij}$ their real cross term. We report the pooled statistic
\begin{equation}
\rho_j=\frac{\sum_i C_{ij}}{\sqrt{(\sum_i P_{ij})(\sum_i G_{ij})}}.
\label{eq:pooled}
\end{equation}
Each frame contributes once, so scenes with more captures contribute more terms and this is not the mean of per-frame correlations. For a single frame, a real scalar band fit leaves
$G_{ij}(1-\rho_{ij}^{2})$; the same identity holds for concatenated bands with one
common scalar. Per-frame oracle PSNR is evaluated separately, not inferred from
pooled $\rho_j$. 

\noindent\textbf{Correlation labels and noise correction.} Compute
$\rho_j^{\mathrm{in}}$ from the scalar-matched input by Eq.~\eqref{eq:pooled}.
Using fixed diagnostic thresholds, label a band \emph{below floor} if
$\rho_j^{\mathrm{in}}<0.20$; otherwise label it \emph{input advantage} if
$\rho_j^{\mathrm{in}}-\rho_j>0.05$, and \emph{no advantage} otherwise.
 Labelled energy shares sum the
model's pooled RGB residual energy in the corresponding bands.
For repeated captures, estimate input noise power $N_j$ from half the power of
the difference between the first two captures of each scene--exposure group,
scale by each frame's squared brightness gain and
pool with the same frame weights as $P_j$, the mean input band power.
The corrected input statistic is
$\rho_j^{\mathrm{in}}/\sqrt{1-N_j/P_j}$.
It assumes additive noise uncorrelated with the reference; $N_j/P_j>0.9$ cells are
unstable. Nonpositive signal power or a corrected magnitude above one makes the
correction invalid; such cells are not clipped into a label. Scene bootstrap draws \nBootN{} resamples of scenes with equal probability and retains
all frames of each, so scenes weigh equally and frames within a scene do not; the frame-weighted and scene-weighted gains are \nFfrW{} and \nFscW{}\,dB.
The correction is a sensitivity analysis under this noise model.

\noindent\textbf{Predictability of the local term.} Log input block mean, input variance and the
reference-normalised output block mean predict within-frame, energy-weighted centred block gains,
cross-fitted by scene so no frame is predicted by its own scene. Adding the log reference mean is a
reference-assisted comparison, unavailable at inference, and a convolutional predictor tests richer
spatial features.

\noindent\textbf{A highlight-anchored correction.} The global term above is an oracle, but the diagnosis makes it
actionable under a checkable precondition. Well-exposed long-exposure references saturate at their upper percentiles: at
$q=\nFQ{}$, \nFsatS{} percent of the Sony training references already reach the 8-bit ceiling, so the anchor is that
ceiling, whose value needs no data. We therefore rescale each output by $1/Q_q(y)$, with $Q_q$ its $q$th
percentile, clamped to $[0.5,2]$, a guard that never binds here; the rule needs no reference, calibration set or per-scene fitting. This is the one-sided,
single-parameter case of percentile-based colour balance~\cite{scb2011}, an upper-percentile anchor of the kind used to estimate atmospheric light~\cite{dcp2011} and, on the input, to amplify it~\cite{irlle2026}; we divide a trained output instead. The precondition is measurable on any benchmark by
the fraction of training references saturated at $q$, and we report the correction where that fraction is high and where it
is not. It is measured on training references and transfers: all \nNSCENE{} Sony test references saturate at \nFQ{} as well. We report its sensitivity to $q$ rather than tuning it: $q=\nFqaK{}$, \nFqbK{}, \nFqcK{}, \nFqdK{}, \nFQ{}, \nFqeK{} and \nFqfK{} give \nFqa{}, \nFqb{}, \nFqc{}, \nFqd{}, \nFRetgain{}, \nFqe{} and \nFqf{}\,dB. Percentiles are non-decreasing in $q$ and every training reference saturates at \nFQ{}, so the precondition holds for every $q\ge\nFQ{}$--against \nFqasat{} to \nFqdsat{} percent saturated at \nFqaK{} to \nFqdK{}--and we take \nFQ{}, the smallest at which every training reference saturates.
